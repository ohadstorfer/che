## 1. `tw-01-fiaca`

> Argentine word: FIACA
> 
> That feeling when you just don't want to do anything.
> 
> "Hoy tengo fiaca." = I feel lazy today.

## 2. `tw-02-laburo`

> In Argentina nobody goes to "trabajo".
> 
> They go to el laburo. 💼
> 
> "Me voy al laburo." = I'm off to work.

## 3. `tw-03-bondi` *(+ link)*

> Bus in Spain: autobús
> Bus in Mexico: camión
> Bus in Argentina: colectivo, or just BONDI 🚌

## 4. `tw-04-mango`

> "No tengo un mango."
> 
> Literally: I don't have a mango.
> Really: I'm broke. 🥭💸

## 5. `tw-05-al-toque`

> Argentine phrase: AL TOQUE
> 
> It means right away.
> 
> "Te llamo al toque." = I'll call you right back.

## 6. `tw-06-de-una`

> Someone in Argentina asks: "¿Vamos por unas birras?"
> 
> The only right answer: "¡De una!" 🍻
> 
> (= Sure, let's go!)

## 7. `tw-07-copado`

> Argentine word: COPADO
> 
> Cool, nice, fun.
> 
> "Tu amigo es re copado." = Your friend is really cool.

## 8. `tw-08-chamuyo` *(+ link)*

> Argentine word: CHAMUYO
> 
> Smooth talk to win someone over. Flirting, or just making things up.
> 
> "Eso es puro chamuyo." = That's all talk.

## 9. `tw-09-trucho`

> Argentine word: TRUCHO
> 
> Fake. Knock-off. Not legit.
> 
> "Esas zapatillas son truchas." = Those sneakers are fake.

## 10. `tw-10-al-horno`

> "Estoy al horno."
> 
> Literally: I'm in the oven. 🔥
> Really: I'm in big trouble.
> 
> Exam tomorrow and you didn't study? Al horno.

## 11. `tw-11-podrido`

> "Estoy podrido."
> 
> Literally: I'm rotten.
> Really: I'm fed up.
> 
> "Estoy podrido del tráfico." = I'm sick of the traffic.

## 12. `tw-12-mufa`

> Argentine word: MUFA
> 
> Bad luck. Or the person who brings it.
> 
> If your team loses every time you watch, sorry: sos mufa. ⚽

## 13. `tw-13-garron`

> Argentine word: GARRÓN
> 
> A bummer. Something that sucks.
> 
> "Se cortó la luz. ¡Qué garrón!" = The power went out. What a bummer!

## 14. `tw-14-embole`

> "¡Qué embole!"
> 
> What Argentines say when something is SO boring.
> 
> Monday meetings? Un embole. 😴

## 15. `tw-15-afano` *(+ link)*

> In Argentina, when the price is way too high:
> 
> "¡Es un afano!"
> 
> = It's a rip-off! 💸

## 16. `tw-16-bancar`

> One verb, two meanings. BANCAR:
> 
> "Te banco." = I've got your back.
> "No lo banco." = I can't stand him.
> 
> Argentine Spanish is wild.

## 17. `tw-17-posta` *(+ link)*

> Argentine word: POSTA
> 
> "¿Posta?" = For real?
> "Posta." = For real.
> 
> Yes, that's where our name comes from. 🇦🇷

## 18. `tw-18-groso`

## 19. `tw-19-flashear`

## 33. `tw-33-vos` *(+ link)*

> In Argentina nobody says "tú".
> 
> They say VOS.
> 
> "Tú tienes" → "vos tenés"
> "Tú eres" → "vos sos"
> "Tú quieres" → "vos querés"

## 34. `tw-34-vos-commands`

> Commands with vos are short and sharp:
> 
> ven → vení
> dime → decime
> mira → mirá
> espera → esperá
> 
> Say them with a little attitude. 🇦🇷

## 35. `tw-35-sos`

> "Sos" isn't a cry for help.
> 
> In Argentina it means "you are".
> 
> "Sos un genio." = You're a genius.

## 36. `tw-36-vos-everyone`

> In Argentina, vos isn't only for close friends.
> 
> Coworkers, the waiter, even your boss: most people use vos with almost everyone.
> 
> And tú? Nobody uses it.

## 37. `tw-37-sh` *(+ link)*

> Why do Argentines sound different?
> 
> LL and Y sound like SH.
> 
> yo → "sho"
> calle → "cashe"
> llave → "shave"
> 
> Once you hear it, you can't unhear it.

## 38. `tw-38-pollo`

> Ask for "pollo" in Buenos Aires and you'll hear it back as "posho". 🐔
> 
> Same chicken. Different sound.

## 39. `tw-39-che`

> CHE is how Argentines call someone.
> 
> It goes first:
> "Che, ¿venís esta noche?" = Hey, are you coming tonight?
> 
> Not at the end. Never "Hola, che."

## 40. `tw-40-re`

> Argentine shortcut: RE
> 
> Put it in front of anything to make it stronger.
> 
> re lindo = really nice
> re caro = super expensive
> re bien = really well

## 41. `tw-41-dale` *(+ link)*

> DALE is the most useful word in Argentina.
> 
> OK? Dale.
> Let's go? Dale.
> Hurry up! ¡Dale!
> See you at 8? Dale.
> 
> One word. Every answer.

## 42. `tw-42-tipo`

> Argentines use TIPO the way English speakers use "like".
> 
> "Llegué tipo a las diez y, tipo, no había nadie."
> 
> = I got there like at ten and, like, nobody was there.

## 43. `tw-43-que-se-yo`

> "Qué sé yo."
> 
> What Argentines say when they have no idea. Or don't want to answer. 🤷
> 
> = Who knows.

## 44. `tw-44-ni-ahi`

>

## 45. `tw-45-nada-que-ver`

> "Nada que ver."
> 
> Literally: nothing to see.

## 46. `tw-46-ojo`

>

## 47. `tw-47-buenas`

## 48. `tw-48-pibe`

## 49. `tw-49-chabon`

> Argentine word: CHABÓN
> 
> A guy. Some dude.
> 
> "Vino un chabón y me preguntó la hora." = Some guy came over and asked me the time.

## 50. `tw-50-cheto`

> Argentine word: CHETO
> 
> Posh, fancy, a bit snobby.
> 
> "Ese barrio es re cheto." = That neighborhood is really fancy.

## 51. `tw-51-dinner` *(+ link)*

> Dinner time in Argentina: 9 pm, 10 pm, sometimes 11.
> 
> If you're eating at 7, you're having a snack. 🍽️

## 52. `tw-52-mate`

> If an Argentine asks "¿Querés tomar unos mates?", say yes. 🧉
> 
> And don't say "gracias" until you're done. "Gracias" means: no more for me.

## 53. `tw-53-boliche`

> In Argentina:
> 
> la previa = drinks at home before going out
> el boliche = the club
> 
> The previa starts at midnight. The boliche at 2 am. 🪩

## 54. `tw-54-facturas`

> In Argentina, "facturas" are not bills.
> 
> They are pastries. 🥐
> 
> Medialunas, vigilantes, bolas de fraile… Sunday morning is for facturas.

## 55. `tw-55-finde`

> "¿Qué hacés el finde?"
> 
> finde = fin de semana = weekend
> 
> Argentines shorten everything: celu, compu, finde, bici.

## 56. `tw-56-asado`

> An asado in Argentina is not just a barbecue.
> 
> It's 5 hours, 12 people, one person at the grill, and a round of applause for the asador at the end. 👏🥩

## 57. `tw-57-besito`

> Meeting someone in Argentina? One kiss on the cheek. 😘
> 
> Men too, between friends.
> 
> No handshake, no two kisses.

## 58. `tw-58-guess-garpar`
