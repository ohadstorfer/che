# Course audit, 2026-09-27

A quick scan of the live course, imagining an English speaker who starts with zero Spanish.

**How it was done**
- Automatic checks over all published content: 381 units, 2,823 lessons, 29,261 sentences and 955 tips.
- Three reviewers read 45 sample units: 3 per section, one of them a practice unit.
- Nothing was changed.

Every item below has three parts: **where** it is, **what's wrong**, and the **fix**.

## 1. 🔴 Wrong Spanish accepted as correct

> ✅ **Fixed 2026-09-27**: rules in `src/lib/course-rules/accept.ts`; all accepted answers regenerated with `scripts/course/regen-answers.mjs` (4,865 sentences updated, 790 broken answers removed).

The accepted answers (`es_alt`) are partly generated by rules. Those rules create broken Spanish, so a learner who writes it gets told they're right.

| Pattern | Example | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Gender flip on a noun, not an adjective | *estudiar inglés* → "estudiar **inglesa**"; *laburo nuevo* → "laburo **nueva**"; *depto luminoso* → "**luminosa**"; *vino riquísimo* → "vino **rica**"; *era carísimo* → "era **cara**" | ~180 for "inglesa" alone; more for the others |
| "vos" inserted twice | "¿**Vos** querés pagar **vos**?", "Prometiste que **vos** ibas a cocinar **vos**" | ~240 |
| Pronoun where Spanish can't have one | "**Ella** se le cayó el mate", "**Él** me parece que no viene" | ~10 |
| Pronoun made unnatural | "¿**Yo** puedo hacer una consulta?" | a few |

**Fix**
1. Stop gender swaps on nouns and language names, and on adjectives that agree with a noun.
2. Never insert "vos" when the sentence already has one.
3. Never insert a subject pronoun before *se le / me parece / me gusta*.
4. Then regenerate `es_alt` for the whole course and delete the bad ones. This is one script run, like `course:answers`.

## 2. 🔴 Correct answers rejected

> ✅ **Fixed 2026-09-27** (same run): gender for "I/we" in more places, *nosotros/nosotras*, *el que/la que*, *Encantado/a*; sentences with identical English now accept each other (957 sentences). Not covered: different English for the same idea (e.g. *debería* for *tendría que*).

### a) Gender

The English doesn't say whether the speaker is a man or a woman, but only one gender is accepted.
- **Examples:** "I was really tired" accepts only *cansada*. "I'm busy because my son is sick" accepts only *ocupado*. Also *encantada*, *cuando era chica*, *atrasado*, *nerviosa*, *nosotras tenemos…*, *soy el que paga*.
- **Size:** about 390 sentences found automatically, perhaps 250 of them real.
- **Why it happens:** the rule skips verbs that could be "I" or "he/she" (*estaba*, *iría*), any sentence that mentions a third person (*mi hijo está enfermo*), plural "we" (*estamos atrasados*), and nouns (*amiga*, *primo/prima*).
- **Fix:**
  - Use the English "I/we/you" to decide that the sentence is about the speaker or the listener.
  - Allow the swap on the speaker's adjective even when a third person appears elsewhere.
  - Allow *nosotros/nosotras*.
  - Add noun pairs (amigo/amiga, el/la que).

### b) Same English, different Spanish, not cross-accepted

- **Size:** 41 such cases were found automatically, and the reviewers found more.
- **Examples:**
  - "Of course I'll help you" is either *Obvio que te ayudo* or *Claro que te ayudo*, and each rejects the other.
  - "Can you hand/pass me…" is either *alcanzás* or *pasás*.
  - "I lost my job" is either *Me quedé sin laburo* or *Perdí el laburo*.
  - "My boss isn't bad" is either *jefe/malo* or *jefa/mala*.
  - "I'm broke" is *Estoy sin un peso* and rejects *sin un mango*.
  - "Sit down, please" has 3 answers: *sentate*, *siéntese* and *siéntense*.
  - Rioplatense *tendría que* ("I should") is taught, but *debería* is rejected.
- **Fix:**
  - Group sentences by identical English and add each one's Spanish to the others' accepted answers. This is automatic and only for exact matches.
  - For "you" sentences, add "(informal)/(formal)" to the English, or accept all forms.

## 3. 🟠 Units drift off their topic

Filler sentences drill an old word instead of the unit's skill.

| Unit | What's there | What's missing |
|---|---|---|
| #47 la-hora (Tell the time) | Mostly ages, blocks, weekdays; fewer than 10 time sentences | *es la una*, *son las…*, *a las…*, *¿a qué hora?* |
| #58 cuanto-sale | ~30 *cuesta*, 3 *sale* | The title phrase itself |
| #96 cuando-era-chico | "Hay muchas escuelas cerca" (present tense) | Imperfect: "Mi escuela era chica" |
| laburo-nuevo | ~20 *volver* and ~10 *malo* sentences | Job talk |
| te-recomiendo | 8 plain "Comprá pan" commands | Advice |
| me-haces-un-favor | ~20 bread/salt sentences | Favors |
| se-me-cayo | ~15 *roto* / glasses sentences | More *se me/te/le* |
| se-alquila | ~12 bike sentences with no *se* | Signs and ads (*se busca*, *se permite*) |
| practica-depende | Headline "Depende de cómo juegue" never drilled; "cuánto cuesta" should be *cueste* | depende + subjunctive |
| se-la-cree | ~20% "La verdad, …" (article *la*, not the clitic) | *la pasé bárbaro*, *la pifié*, *me la jugué* |
| no-es-que | ~⅓ other subjunctive triggers | More "No es que…, es que…" |
| como-dice-el-dicho | Only 3 sayings; "como dice el dicho" never used | Classic porteño sayings |
| hay-paro | wifi/pills "no funciona" filler | Strike vocabulary |
| practica-la-guita | Summary copied from dicen-que | Money summary |

**Fix:** the same pattern likely repeats across the course.
- Run an automatic check of what share of each unit's sentences use the unit's own grammar focus or new words, and flag units under ~60%.
- Top up the flagged units with the existing writer/judge loop, retiring the filler.

## 4. 🟠 Advanced units with beginner content

**Fix:** same check as #3, plus a minimum sentence length or difficulty for sections 11+.

## 7. 🟡 Word glosses

| Problem | Count | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conjugated verb glossed as infinitive | 185 of 1,424 | *recomiendo* = "to recommend", *rompió* = "to break", *pasás* = "to pass, to come in" | "I recommend", "(it) broke", "you pass" |

## Other notes

- **Thin units:** #5 vos-y-sos has 8 sentences and #10 de-donde-sos has 11 (most units have 60+). Top them up.
- **Unused words:**
  - *boludo* and *quilombo* (#69), *Recoleta*, *Obelisco* and *gramo* appear in 0 sentences.
  - *boludo* and *quilombo* are blocked by the register ceiling. This is still an open decision.
- **Slang before the standard word:**
  - *profe* (#6) comes before *profesor* (#40), and *celu* (#27) before *celular* (#130).
  - This is fine for a porteño course, but a tip could say it's short for the full word.
- **Validator noise:** 1,004 "length.band" warnings, where sentences are longer than their difficulty level allows. Either loosen the band or recompute difficulty from length, so real warnings stand out.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Fix the answer rules, then regenerate all accepted answers** (items 1, 2a, 9). This removes the biggest unfairness in one pass and costs no agents.
2. **Cross-accept identical English** (2b). Automatic.
3. **Fix the wrong tips and add the missing ones** (item 6). Small and high value.
4. **Gloss pass** for conjugated verbs and plurals (item 7).
5. **Add an "on-topic share" check, then top up the flagged units** (items 3–5). This is the biggest job, using the writer/judge loop.
6. Specific sentence fixes (item 8) and the thin units.

*This was a quick scan: 45 of 381 units were read by hand. Items 3–8 are probably repeated in units that weren't read.*
