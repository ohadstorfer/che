### 0:00 — Why Argentine Spanish is different

**[Cuadro 1: globos que aparecen de a uno]**

> **(en castellano, rápido)** "Che, ¿vos tenés ganas de ir a tomar algo? Sho invito."
>
> If you've studied Spanish, there's a good chance half of that just went over your head. And that's not your fault.
>
> Argentine Spanish plays by its own rules. People say **vos** instead of tú. They pronounce "yo" like **sho**, and "calle" like **cashe**. And there's a whole layer of slang called **lunfardo**: words like **laburo** for work, **mina** for woman, **boludo** for… well, it depends on the tone.
>
> So if you want to talk to Argentines, and understand them, you can't just learn "Spanish". You have to learn *this* Spanish. Here are the five steps that actually work.

### 0:45 — Step 1: Learn vos from day one

**[Cuadro 2: tachado sobre "tú tienes"; la lista se arma palabra por palabra]**

> Step one: learn **vos** from day one.
>
> A lot of people learn tú first and plan to "switch later". Don't. It's double the work, and in Argentina you'll sound like you learned from a TV dub.
>
> The good news: vos is easy. In the present tense, take the infinitive, swap the R for an S, and stress the last vowel.
>
> **Tener** → **tenés**. **Querer** → **querés**. **Poder** → **podés**. **Vivir** → **vivís**.
>
> The one irregular you need is **ser**: **vos sos**.
>
> That's it. Five minutes and you already sound more Argentine than most textbooks.

**[Cuadro 3: grabación real de Posta: clase → slang → Hablar]**

### 2:15 — Step 3: YouTube

**[Cuadro 4: pantalla de video con subtítulos en español]**

> Step three: YouTube. But not videos *about* Spanish. Videos *in* Argentine Spanish.
>
> Argentina has a huge streaming culture. Channels like **Olga** and **Luzu TV** are basically live talk shows with friends joking around, which is exactly how people really talk.
>
> Here's the trick: turn on Spanish subtitles and drop the speed to **0.75**. When a word comes up a lot, look it up once. You'll be surprised how fast "¿viste?", "re" and "posta" stop sounding like noise.

### 3:00 — Step 4: Netflix

**[Cuadro 5: Pancho en el sillón con un mate, subtítulos EN → ES]**

> Step four: Netflix, with a system.
>
> Pick an Argentine series. Some good ones: **División Palermo** if you like dry comedy, **Envidiosa** if you like drama with jokes, **El Eternauta** if you want sci-fi, and **Casi feliz** if you want everyday Buenos Aires life.
>
> Watch each episode twice. The first time, with English subtitles, so you follow the story. The second time, with Spanish subtitles, so you catch the words.
>
> The second time is where the learning happens, because now you know what they mean and you can focus on how they say it.

### 3:45 — Step 5: Private classes

**[Cuadro 6: videollamada con un profe argentino]**

> Step five: private classes, but with the right teacher.
>
> Look for an **Argentine** teacher online. Not a "neutral Spanish" teacher. You want someone who says vos without thinking about it.
>
> And use the class for one thing: talking. Don't pay someone to explain grammar you can get from an app. Thirty minutes of conversation a week is worth more than two hours of worksheets.
>
> **Classes for speaking. The app for vocabulary.** That's the combination.

### 4:30 — Your weekly plan

**[Cuadro 7: plan semanal → end card de Posta]**
